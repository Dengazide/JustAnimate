# The London Beer Flood (30-second original short)

## Narration

London once had a fifteen-foot wave of beer sweep through its streets.
It's October 17, 1814, in St Giles.
At the Horse Shoe Brewery, a twenty-two-foot vat holds thousands of barrels of porter.
One enormous iron hoop slips off.
An hour later, the vat bursts.
Its force opens nearby vats and smashes through the brewery wall.
A dark wave floods narrow streets, cellars and homes.
Eight people are killed; a ninth dies the next day from alcohol poisoning.
More than 323,000 imperial gallons spill out.
And the brewery? It soon starts brewing again.
Today, the Dominion Theatre stands near the site.

## Sources and fact notes
- Historic England records 323,000+ imperial gallons and nine deaths in total, including one later alcohol-poisoning death.
- A hoop slipped off the 22-foot vat about an hour before the rupture; the exact mechanism is described differently across accounts.
- The 15-foot wave height is an estimate in historical summaries.
- All visible characters are fictional composites, not victims.
